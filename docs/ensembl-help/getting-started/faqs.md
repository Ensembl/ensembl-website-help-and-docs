# Frequently Asked Questions

## Ensembl releases and versions

**Why is there a new Ensembl?**
- As described in our [Ensembl 2026](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/54/D1/D1053/8343504) publication, our rate of genome annotation and release has rapidly increased over the past few years. Our new platform helps us to share more genome data and provide new opportunities for genomic insights and discoveries.

**Why am I being redirected from the Ensembl Archive to the new Ensembl website?**

- If you are being redirected from the Ensembl Archive to the new Ensembl website, your browser may be using cached data from a previous visit. Clearing your browser cache should resolve the issue.
* How to clear your browser cache:
1. Open your browser settings.
2. Find the **Privacy**, **Privacy & Security**, or **Clear browsing data** section.
3. Select the option to clear **cached images and files**. You usually do not need to clear your passwords or other saved data.
4. Close and reopen your browser.
5. Try accessing the Ensembl Archive again.
6. If the redirect persists: search the browser settings for site specific data, locate ensembl.org in the list of sites, and select the option to clear its cookies and site data.

**Keyboard shortcut**

You can also use the following shortcut in most browsers:

- **Windows/Linux:** `Ctrl + Shift + Delete`
- **macOS:** `Command + Shift + Delete`

> If the issue persists after clearing your cache or deleting the site's data, please contact the Ensembl Helpdesk for assistance.
   

**How can I access the previous Ensembl or older releases?**

- Previous Ensembl versions and data releases are available from our [archives](https://ensembl.org/help/articles/archives).

**Are all the same features on the new Ensembl as are on the legacy sites?** 
- No, the new site is still in active development. Many new features and data pipelines are coming in future releases. Our most recent [archives](https://ensembl.org/help/articles/archives) of the legacy site will remain live with tool support to allow for coverage as new features are launched. We post feature updates on our blog: [ensembl.info](https://ensembl.info) and for summer 2026 we list a [feature profile](https://www.ensembl.info/2026/07/21/feature-profile-between-new-and-legacy-ensembl-july-2026-update/) comparing this site to the most recent legacy versions. 

**What is a release and what types of release are there?**

- A release refers to a new genome or an updated genome which includes updates to genesets, variation data, regulatory data or comparative genomic analysis. There are two types of releases, [partial and integrated releases](https://ensembl.org/help/articles/partial-and-integrated-data-releases). Partial releases are data releases where new data is added to the site. Integrated releases incorporate partial releases together linking those genome data and analyses at a fixed point in time. 

**Are partial releases incomplete?**
- Partial releases are complete data releases. They are partial in respect to the previously integrated genomes on the site. 

**Which release type is suitable for long term use?**

- Integrated releases are best for long term use as the data is linked and fixed at a point in time. Partial releases may be superseded due to errors found or new data released before integration.  

## Genome and species availability

**Does this site support only vertebrates?**

- No, this site supports genomes from across the tree of life. We display genomes and annotations the Ensembl Genomes sites for vertebrates, plants, fungi, bacteria, metazoa and protists. 

**Will you support all the same genomes as before?**
- We aim to support the vast majority of genomes as we have before, and will add more species and pangenomes to the new site. 


**What human assemblies are available?**
- The current human reference genome, GRCh38 is the most comprehensively annotated human genome on this site. The previous version of the human genome GRCh37 is also available. The complete telomere to telomere assembly of a human genome: T2T-CHM13v2.0 is available with [annotation mapped](https://ensembl.org/help/articles/human-genome-automated-annotation) from GRCh38. There are many alternative human haplotypes available to browse from the Human Pangenome Reference Consortium. 

## Sequence display information

**How can I download gene sequences?**
- You can download gene sequences from the [Genome browser and Feature explorer apps](https://ensembl.org/help/articles/get-sequences). Clicking on genes will open up a pop-up box, and offer a “Downloads” drop-down where you can select which sequence you would like to download in FASTA format. 


**How can I see exon and intron sequences for a transcript?**
- Access these sequences within the Exon display of Feature explorer.

**Can I see markup on sequence?**
- Not yet. A new app focused on sequence data is in development. You can access this feature on our legacy sites in our [archives](https://ensembl.org/help/articles/archives).

**How to view the sequence of a gene or transcript in the site?**
- When viewing a gene in the Genome browser app, “More information” in the right-hand side panel of the Genome browser can be accessed for a gene or transcript by clicking “...”. This will open up a display with a drop-down menu for “Sequence”, which then can be copied, downloaded, or used in BLAST. 

## Other data 

**How do I cite Ensembl?**
- You can cite Ensembl with our most recent overview article in our [publications list](https://ensembl.org/about/articles/citing-ensembl). We encourage you to list the release date e.g. 2026_07 - integrated to allow others to find the data you used.
